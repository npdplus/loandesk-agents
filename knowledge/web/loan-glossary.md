# SME Working Capital Loan — Glossary

**Contoso Bank (fictional).** This document is part of a demo. Contoso Bank, its products, rates and policies
are not real. It is read through a Copilot connector (Enterprise websites), not through the search indexes.

Plain-language meanings of the words used in the loan documents, in alphabetical order.

**Annual revenue** — The money the business received from sales in its latest full financial year, before
costs. The loan can be at most 30 % of it.

**BOJ.5** — The list of shareholders of a Thai limited company, issued by the Department of Business
Development. A limited company sends one issued within the last 3 months.

**Commercial registration certificate** — The registration document of a sole proprietor or a partnership
that is not a company. It shows that the business is registered in Thailand.

**Credit officer** — The Contoso Bank employee who reviews an application and decides it. The LoanDesk
assistant never decides.

**Equal monthly instalment** — The same payment every month for the whole term. Each payment covers some of
the loan and the interest for that month.

**Fixed rate** — An interest rate that does not change during the term of the loan.

**Front-end fee** — A one-time fee taken from the loan amount when the loan is paid out.

**Guarantor** — The owner or a director who promises to repay the loan personally if the business cannot.

**Needs information** — An application status. The bank needs something more before it can decide; it tells
the customer what.

**PND.90** — The Thai personal income tax return. A sole proprietor sends the last 2 years.

**PP.20** — The Thai VAT registration certificate. Only a business registered for VAT has one.

**Pre-check** — The quick check of the basic rules that the LoanDesk assistant does before an application. A
pre-check is not a loan decision.

**Reducing balance** — Interest is calculated each month on the part of the loan that is still unpaid, so the
interest part of each instalment gets smaller over time.

**Term** — How long the business has to repay the loan: 6, 12, 18, 24 or 36 months.

**Working capital** — The money a business uses for day-to-day needs, such as stock, salaries and supplier
bills.
